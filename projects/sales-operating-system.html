Sales Operating System

A target-driven Sales Operating System built for a leading medical equipment distributor in South India — replacing activity-tracking CRM habits with a closed loop from sales target to realised revenue.

Explore Architecture System Overview

Overview

The Challenge

Selling capital medical equipment is not a volume game. A single deal — an imaging system, a set of ICU monitors, an operating-theatre upgrade — can take months, and the decision sits with a committee: clinicians, biomedical engineers, purchase managers and administrators, each with different priorities. Traditional CRMs respond by asking reps to log every call and visit. The result is plenty of activity data and very little answer to the question leadership actually cares about: are we on track to hit this quarter's target, and where will the shortfall come from?

The Solution

The Sales OS is organised around the target, not the activity log. Every quarter starts with a target, cascades to the hospitals that must be covered to reach it, flows into the deals being worked at those hospitals, and closes with revenue measured against the original target. Activity is still captured — but as evidence feeding that loop, not as an end in itself.

Core Operating Framework

  • Target Planning: Company targets cascade through business units down to individual reps, split by brand and tracked quarter by quarter.
  • Account Coverage: Planning which hospitals each rep must cover each quarter to reach target — currently rolling out.
  • Opportunity Pipeline: Leads become deals that move through defined stages with gate checks; winning or losing is recorded as an outcome, kept separate from the pipeline stage.
  • Projects & Tenders: A hospital expansion or a state government procurement tender is modelled as a project that groups several related deals.
  • Stakeholder-First Intelligence: Sentiment (Promoter / Neutral / Detractor) is tracked for each person on the buying committee, not just for the hospital as a whole.
  • Customer 360: Each hospital's purchase history and installed equipment in a single view.

Key Differentiators

  • Quota-driven, not activity-driven: Every screen answers "how does this move us toward target?"
  • Business-unit isolation in the database: Two SBUs share one system, and each sees only its own data — enforced by PostgreSQL Row-Level Security, not just hidden on screen.
  • Governed pipeline: Stage gates, with a manager-attested override when a deal legitimately needs to skip a step; every change to key records is captured in an audit trail.
  • Rep judgement plus system logic: Win probability combines a systematic, stage-based value with the rep's own override.

The Sales Process

The system runs as a closed loop: each stage feeds the next, and results flow back into the next quarter's planning.

The Operating Loop

Target

Company → SBU → Rep, split by brand

Coverage Rolling out

Leads → staged deals, grouped into projects & tenders

Revenue

Won deals measured against target in role-based dashboards

Won and Lost are outcomes, not pipeline stages — so a deal's stage history stays intact whatever the result.

System Architecture

A React web app, a Python FastAPI layer for business rules and workflows, and a PostgreSQL database on Supabase with Row-Level Security.
